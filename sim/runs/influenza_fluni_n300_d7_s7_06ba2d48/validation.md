## Notes on published rates

- **sr_mitchell2011_stay_home** (US university students with acute respiratory infection (ARI) during the April-May 2009 pH1N1 outbreak at the University of Delaware (n=423 students with ARI)): Abstract: 'only 27 (6.4%) of 423 students ... with acute respiratory infection (ARI) reported staying home while ill', even though the university recommended home isolation. Self-report online survey. DOI 10.1093/cid/ciq056.
- **sr_mitchell2011_social_events_while_ill** (US university students with ARI, Univ. of Delaware, 2009 pH1N1 outbreak): Abstract: 'Nearly one-half (46%) of student respondents, including 44.7% of those with ARI, attended social events.' Use as a carry_on check for social activities while sick. DOI 10.1093/cid/ciq056.
- **sr_katz2012_vaden** (Undergraduate/graduate students at a large urban US university (George Washington Univ.) reporting ILI, survey Nov 2009 (n=819 respondents; 16.7% response rate)): Results ('Self-reported ILI and Associated Behaviors'): '85 (38.6% of respondents) sought medical care.' The implied denominator (220) does not match the 262 ILI cases reported elsewhere in the paper. DOI 10.1080/07448481.2011.570398.
- **sr_katz2012_missed_no_class** (Students with a single ILI episode at a large urban US university, Nov 2009): Results text: 'Just over a third of students reporting a single episode of ILI also reported missing no work or school (66, 33%)'; Table 4 shows 66 (35.5%) for the same group, so the paper conflicts. 'Missed no class' is the closest measure of full presenteeism. Abstract: 'almost no student self-isolated for the entire duration of their illness.'
- **sr_biggerstaff2014_vaden_18_49** (US adults aged 18-49 with self-reported ILI, BRFSS Jan-Apr 2011 (n=2390)): Table 1: 42.7% (95% CI 37.4-48.1) of 18-49-year-olds with ILI sought healthcare (all adults 45.2%). General-population care seeking, not student health: students with a free on-campus clinic may differ. DOI 10.1093/infdis/jiu224.
- **sr_van2010_attend_if_flu_symptoms** (University students, UNSW Sydney, Australia, Jun-Sep 2009 pH1N1 (n=1876 students)): Table 4 ('Intended behaviour at the University', stem 'I would come into university if...'): 'symptoms consistent with flu, e.g. fever and a cough/runny nose' = 407/1876 (21.7%) of students. This is a hypothetical intention, not observed behavior. The Results text gives '75%, 1407/1876', which does not match the table and is probably a typo.
- **sr_van2010_attend_if_flu_and_exam** (University students, UNSW Sydney, 2009 (n=1876)): Table 4: would come in with flu symptoms 'and I had an exam/assessment due' = 1248/1876 (66.5%) of students, versus 43.1% of academic staff. Intention only. Useful for checking that academic pressure raises carry_on.
- **sr_smith2021_full_self_isolation** (UK residents aged 16+ with COVID-19 symptoms, 37 national surveys Mar 2020-Jan 2021 (n=1213 symptomatic)): Abstract: duration-adjusted adherence to full self-isolation 42.5% (39.7-45.2), latest wave 51.8% (40.8-62.8). Intention to self-isolate was about 70%, so stated intention far exceeds behavior. Non-adherence was associated with younger age and male sex. COVID-era legal and policy context. DOI 10.1136/bmj.n608 (corrected version notes main findings unchanged).
- **sr_smith2021_test_request** (UK residents aged 16+ with COVID-19 symptoms, Mar 2020-Jan 2021 (n=3068)): Abstract: 'requesting a test for covid-19 was 18.0% (16.6% to 19.3%)', rising to 22.2% in Jan 2021. This is test seeking when symptomatic, a proxy for care seeking. DOI 10.1136/bmj.n608.
- **nr_katz2012_followed_stay_home_advice** (Students with ILI at a large urban US university who were told to stay home by a physician, professor or university website (n=107), Nov 2009): Results: '107 (43.1%) were told ... to stay at home' and '96 of those students (89.7%) followed the advice, at least partially.' 'At least partially' means this is an upper bound on full compliance; the paper says most did not isolate as long as recommended.
- **nr_steens2020_quarantine_isolation_overall** (Adults in Norway requested to quarantine/isolate (mandatory), Apr-Jul 2020 (n=1704)): Abstract: self-reported adherence to requested measures 42% (95% CI 37-48); 66% in April, falling to 33-38% in May-July. The fall over time is also a check on decay. Manual tracing, not an app. DOI 10.2807/1560-7917.ES.2020.25.37.2001607.
- **nr_steens2020_adherence_symptomatic** (Adults in Norway requested to quarantine/isolate who had COVID-19-compatible symptoms, 2020): Abstract: adherence 'higher among those experiencing COVID-19-compatible symptoms (71%) compared with those without (28%)'. This is the best available severity/symptom modifier: having symptoms roughly 2.5x the adherence.
- **nr_steens2020_adherence_asymptomatic** (Adults in Norway requested to quarantine/isolate without symptoms, 2020): Abstract: adherence 28% among those without symptoms. Pair with nr_steens2020_adherence_symptomatic.
- **ac_baseman2022_tested** (WA Notify (Google/Apple EN) users in Washington State who received an exposure notification, follow-up survey Jan-Jun 2021 (n=407; 34.6% aged 18-34)): Abstract: 'Of 407 respondents to the follow-up survey, 57.5% (n = 234) reported getting tested.' Follow-up responders are a self-selected subset of the 1507 initial responders, so this likely overstates testing. At notification only 40.1% (604/1507) intended to get tested. DOI 10.1177/00333549221110301.
- **ac_baseman2022_stayed_home** (WA Notify users who received an exposure notification, follow-up survey (n=407)): Results text: 'Stayed home until I got test results' 39.1% (n=159). Other behaviors, including avoiding public places for 10-14 days, appear only in Figure 2 and are not transcribed here.
- **ac_ebbers2021_home_quarantine** (Dutch CoronaMelder app users who received an exposure notification, Oct-Nov 2020 survey (n=722; ages 15+)): Report p.13 (section 6, 'Reactie op melding'): after notification respondents went into home quarantine 40% (291/722). Of those who quarantined, 62% started immediately. p.17: 45% said they 'always' stayed home. Young people stayed home 'always' significantly less often than older people (p.17). In Dutch; non-peer-reviewed government-commissioned evaluation.
- **ac_ebbers2021_test** (Dutch CoronaMelder users who received a notification (n=722)): Report p.13: 'De meeste mensen vroegen een test aan (41% als ondergrens...)' ('most people requested a test (41% as a lower bound)'). 12% did nothing; 20% contacted the public health service (GGD) and 12% their GP. Symptomatic recipients did not request tests significantly more often (46% vs 40%).
- **ac_ballouz2022_tested_after_en** (Adult close contacts in the Zurich SARS-CoV-2 cohort who got a SwissCovid exposure notification before manual tracing (n=18), Aug 2020-Jan 2021): Abstract: 'Of the 18 contacts receiving an EN before MCT, 14 (77.8%) took recommended measures: 12 (66.7%) were tested'; 7 (38.9%) called the infoline. The sample is very small, hence the wide tolerance. Preventive action was 82% in non-household versus 67% in household exposures. DOI 10.2196/35653.
- **ac_daniore2021_any_preventive_action** (SwissCovid exposure-notification recipients (Switzerland), review of empirical studies 2020-2021): Abstract: 'SwissCovid exposure notifications contributed to preventive actions in 76% of exposure notification recipients'. This is a review-level summary, and 'preventive action' is broad (testing, quarantine, calling the infoline). DOI 10.4414/smw.2021.w30031.
- **ac_citron2024_contact_time_after_en** (Anonymous app users in a US university community, Jan-Mar 2021 (pre-vaccine)): Not a rate. Abstract: 'we observed no statistically significant change in contact time before vs. after exposure notification', and no difference versus matched non-notified users. Users who self-reported a positive test cut total contact time with other users by 29% (p=0.002) in the following 7 days. Implication: notified-but-negative students in the sim should not leave or avoid locations much; positives should cut contact by about 30%. Preprint; abstract read via Europe PMC.
- **di_masel2021_share_positive_in_app** (SARS-CoV-2-positive persons at the University of Arizona (mostly students) who had the Covid Watch EN app, Sep-Nov 2020 (157/286)): Abstract: 46% (286/628) of infected persons interviewed had the app, and '55% (157/286) of these app users shared their positive SARS-CoV-2 test result in the app prior to the case investigation interview'. That is 25% of all interviewed cases. Campus setting, closest to Stanford. Abstract read via Europe PMC.
- **di_ebbers2021_share_key** (Dutch CoronaMelder users who tested positive after a notification (n=35)): Report p.17 (section 10): 'De meeste personen die positief testten deelden de sleutel (89%; 31 van de 35)' ('most people who tested positive shared their key (89%; 31 of 35)'). The sample is very small and selected (already-engaged users).
- **di_levy2022_not_disclose_screening** (US adults, national nonprobability online panel, Dec 2021 (item n=945; 26% aged 18-29)): Results: not disclosing possible COVID-19 when screened to enter a clinician's office, 193/945 (20.4%). Ages 18-29 had 4.87x the odds (95% CI 3.27-7.34) of at least one misrepresentation/nonadherence versus 60+, so young adults are likely higher than this pooled rate. DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.35837.
- **di_levy2022_avoid_testing** (US adults who thought they might have COVID-19, Dec 2021 (n=814)): Results: avoided testing when they thought they might have COVID-19, 171/814 (21.0%).
- **di_levy2022_break_quarantine** (US adults who had been required to quarantine, Dec 2021 (n=845)): Abstract: 'breaking quarantine rules (190 of 845 [22.5%])'. Also 24.3% told others they took more precautions than they did. Overall 721/1733 (41.6%) reported at least one of 9 misrepresentation/nonadherence behaviors (abstract), so 1 - 0.416 is a rough ceiling on 'fully honest' agents.
- **di_lash2021_named_contacts** (All lab-confirmed COVID-19 cases reported to 14 US health departments, Jun-Oct 2020 (n=74,185)): Abstract: '43 931 (59%) were interviewed, and 24 705 (33%) named any contacts'. The 33% is of all cases and combines unreachability with non-disclosure. DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.15850.
- **di_smith2021_intend_share_contacts** (UK residents aged 16+, 37 national surveys 2020-2021 (n=45,680)): Abstract: intention to share details of close contacts 79.1% (78.8-79.5). Intention only; compare with di_lash2021_named_contacts for the intention-behavior gap.
- **di_katz2012_notify_professor** (Students with ILI at a large urban US university, Nov 2009): Comment section: 'half of all students with ILI did not notify their professors or ask to be excused'. The rate is the paper's 'half', not a precise count. Results: 119 (49.8%) did not ask professors for extra time or help. Disclosure to instructors, not to friends.
- **in_rao2007_peer_vaccination** (Harvard undergraduates, random residential-house assignment, 2003-2005 flu seasons): Not a rate (a marginal effect). Abstract: 'A student becomes up to 8.3 percentage points more likely to get immunized if an additional 10 percent of her friends receive flu shots.' Also: assignment to a house hosting a flu clinic raised vaccination by 15 percentage points. Elite-university undergraduates (close to Stanford). Working paper, not peer reviewed. Use for peer-influence strength: about +0.083 per +10% of friends adopting.
- **in_ebbers2021_warn_others** (Dutch CoronaMelder users after receiving an exposure notification (n=722)): Report p.13: 'Een kleinere groep heeft mensen in de omgeving gewaarschuwd (28%)' ('a smaller group warned people around them (28%)'). This is warning after an exposure notification, not after a positive test.
- **in_dryhurst2020_personal_experience** (National samples in 10 countries (US, UK, etc.), Mar 2020 (N=6991)): Not a rate. Abstract: personal experience with the virus and 'hearing about the virus from friends and family' were significant predictors of risk perception, and 'social amplification through friends and family' was significant in more than half of countries. Supports direction only (friend infected -> higher worry). Publisher page returned 403; abstract read via OpenAlex/Crossref metadata.
- **wo_rubin2010_worry_peak** (UK residents aged 16+, 36 weekly national telephone surveys May 2009-Jan 2010 (about 1100 per wave)): Abstract: 'The percentage of very or fairly worried participants fluctuated between 9.6% and 32.9%', tracking media volume during the first summer wave. Use 32.9% as the peak; the 9.6% trough is the floor for a mild flu pandemic. Only 1.6% sought medical advice and 2.0% avoided public transport (early May 2009). DOI 10.3310/hta14340-03.
- **wo_bults2011_anxiety_may** (Dutch adults 18+, internet panel, survey 1, 30 Apr-4 May 2009 (n=456)): Table 2: high perceived anxiety 61% (May) -> 40% (June) -> 39%/36% (Aug follow-ups, survey 3.1/3.2). Perceived severity (high) 64% -> 53% -> 29%. Perceived vulnerability rose 15% -> 31% and any preventive measure taken rose 11% -> 40% as cases rose. Anxiety fell about a third in 6 weeks, then plateaued.
- **wo_bults2011_anxiety_june** (Dutch adults 18+, survey 2, 15-19 Jun 2009 (n=478)): Table 2: high anxiety 40% about 6 weeks after the first survey.
- **wo_savadori2022_young_adult_decline** (Italian young adults (mean age 26, range 18-45), two waves Mar 13 and Jul 2020 (n=538)): Not a rate (effect sizes). From epidemic to post-epidemic wave, affective risk perception fell (d about -0.23 to -0.26), analytic risk perception fell more steeply (d about -0.54 to -0.70), social distancing fell (d about -0.17 to -0.59), and hygiene rose. Direction check: worry and distancing should decay after the peak while cheap habits (hygiene, masks) persist. DOI 10.1016/j.socscimed.2022.114949.
- **wo_petherick2021_fatigue** (Representative samples in 14 countries (N=238,797), Mar-Dec 2020): Not a rate. Abstract: mask wearing (low-cost, habituating) rose linearly, while physical distancing (high-cost) declined, with the decline decelerating over time and small rebounds later. No half-life is reported. DOI 10.1038/s41562-021-01181-x.
- **ma_barrios2021_indoor_mandate** (Persons observed at 6 US universities with mask mandates (mostly students), Sep-Nov 2020 (17,200 observations)): Direct observation. Results: 'mask use was significantly more common indoors (94.0%) than outdoors (67.6%)'. Indoors on campus 94.8%, nearby off campus 90.6% (Table 2). Overall correct use 76.7%. DOI 10.15585/mmwr.mm7006e1.
- **ma_barrios2021_outdoor** (Persons observed outdoors at 6 US universities, Sep-Nov 2020): Outdoors, masks were mandated only when distancing could not be kept: 67.6% wore masks.
- **ma_condra2023_campus_vs_community** (Persons observed at East Tennessee State University (campus mandate), CDC MASCUP! 2021): Abstract: adherence on campus 86% versus 72% at off-campus community sites. On-campus adherence changed when the county mandate expired despite the continuing university mandate. Shows that weaker norms or advice lower masking by roughly 14 percentage points. DOI 10.1007/s10900-023-01187-8.
- **ma_katz2012_ili_no_mandate** (Students with ILI at a large urban US university, Nov 2009 (no mask mandate)): Rate left null because the paper reports only subgroups. Table 3: mask use among students with ILI was 3.1%-5.9% across four subgroups. Face masks were among the least-used behaviors. A pre-COVID baseline: symptomatic students with no mask norm almost never mask (simulated rate should be <=0.10).
