Who has the flu? A whole-campus mean-field

Every student's probability of having the flu, updated after every meeting. It uses Baker et al. (PNAS 2021)'s mean-field method, with λj→i from the exposure calculator. The model sees only what phones could see: contacts, self-reports and tests. Rings show who is really sick in the sim. The model never sees them.

Meetings that raised their risk most

Each row shows every input to λj→i, then how much that meeting added. λ is the chance the meeting infects them if the other person is infected. The added risk is P(they were still healthy) × P(other has flu) × λ.

Method

Baker, Biazzo, Braunstein, Catania, Dall'Asta, Ingrosso, Krzakala, Mazza, Mézard, Muntoni, Refinetti, Sarao Mannelli and Zdeborová. "Epidemic mitigation by statistical inference from contact tracing data." PNAS 118 (32), 2021.

P_S^i(t+1) = P_S^i(t) · Π_j [ 1 − Σ_a P_j(age a, t) · λ_j→i(a, t) ] · (1 − γ·Δt) λ_j→i(a, t) = 1 − exp(−q(a) · B · T · ( 1/(Q_near · d²) + 1/(V · loss) )) indoors = 1 − exp(−q(a) · B · T / (Q_near · d² · 5)) outdoors loss = ACH + settling + virus decay(indoor RH(outdoor RH, outdoor °C)) q(a) = scale · quanta_peak · shedding(a − onset)
